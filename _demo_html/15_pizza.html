Exercise 1 · Daily BriefingResearcher · Critique

How a GM starts the day in 2 minutes. Open Microsoft 365 Copilot at copilot.microsoft.com → click Researcher. Keep the model picker on Auto (this is Critique — dual-model with self-review). Paste the prompt below.

Researcher reads your inbox, calendar, Teams chats, and recent files — then hands General Manager · Operations & Marketing a single, structured briefing for the day. No more spending the first 30 minutes catching up.

Task 1.1🔍 Researcher · Auto (Critique)

Generate today's GM daily briefing

Create a daily briefing from emails, messages, documents, and meetings to help me prepare for the current workday. Use your understanding of the projects I work on, workstreams I care about, and key people I work with to understand what is relevant and important. Sections: A short intro paragraph that provides an overview of my day, i.e. what a personal assistant would tell me to greet me in the morning. Key updates since yesterday – Include informational updates from the last workday that are likely to impact my work and collaborations. Suggested tasks for today – Tasks or questions assigned to me that I should handle today. Cross-check that suggested tasks haven't been completed already. List unread email threads from the past week that likely need my reply—prioritize those where I'm on To/CC, @mentioned, or involve requests, deadlines, leadership, or customers. Exclude automated messages. Show only urgent or important items in a concise table, and draft replies where needed. If none require action, say: 'I didn't find any unread threads that require a reply.' Meeting prep for today – Upcoming meetings only, shown in a table with: Meeting (time, title, organizer), Topic Overview, and Suggested Preparations. Note if inferred or not needed. Rules: Start with a header: Daily briefing – Day, Date. Follow with a sub-header stating the timestamp of generation. Start each section with a meaningful emoji. Use only explicitly stated facts; no speculation, interpretations or predictions. If a section has no data, state that.
What success looks like: within 90 seconds you have a one-screen brief: overview paragraph, key updates since yesterday, today's tasks, unread threads that need a reply (with drafts), and a meeting-prep table — all grounded on real signals from your tenant, with citations.
Bonus🔍 Researcher · follow-up

Drill in on the single most important item

Pick one item from the briefing that needs deeper thinking. Ask Researcher:

Take the highest-priority item from the briefing above. Pull together everything I've received on it in the last 14 days — emails, chats, files, meeting notes. Summarise the current state in 5 bullets, list the open decisions, and draft one short message I should send today to move it forward.

Exercise 3 - Market Intelligence

When to do this: Right after the daily briefing, when the GM needs deep market intelligence. Open Microsoft 365 Copilot → Researcher.

3.1 · Critique (Auto) — the default for sector intelligence

Researcher on Auto runs Critique mode — a dual-model setup where one model drafts, a second self-reviews the sources, and only the verified report is returned.

Task 3.1🔍 Researcher · Auto (Critique)

Scan the Quick-Service Restaurant landscape in Indonesia

Research the Quick-Service Restaurant sector in Indonesia. Benchmark these companies: Pizza Hut Indonesia, Domino's Pizza ID, Pizza Marzano, McDonald's ID, KFC Indonesia, Burger King ID. For each one, compare market share, FY25 revenue growth, EBITDA margin, strategic focus, digital maturity, and recent material moves (M&A, capex, new contracts, regulation). Include 2024–2028 market sizing with the three main growth drivers. Cite every source. Flag any section where the evidence is thin.
Notice the citations and the "reviewed by" stamp — Critique enforces source grounding before the report is shown.

3.2 · Model Council — when the decision is too important for one model

Switch the Researcher model picker to Model Council. Multiple frontier models reason on the same question in parallel — Researcher then reconciles them and shows where they agree, where they diverge, and which sources each one anchored on.

Task 3.2🧑‍⚖️ Researcher · Model Council

Stress-test the strategic question

Model Council — for General Manager · Operations & Marketing at Contoso Pizza Co: what are the three highest-impact strategic moves we should make in the next 12 months in the Indonesian quick-service restaurant sector, given the peer benchmark above? For each move, give: the rationale, the evidence (cited), the main risk, and a 90-day starter action. Show me where the models agree, where they disagree, and which one I should trust on each move.
Why Model Council matters for GMs: for any decision worth more than ~USD 1M, the cost of one model being wrong dwarfs the cost of asking three. Model Council surfaces the disagreement instead of hiding it.

3.3 · Turn the research into a 1-page GM brief

Task 3.3📝 Word Copilot · 1-page brief

Export and refine in Word

In the Researcher report, click Export → Convert to Word. Open the file from OneDrive. In the Word Copilot pane, paste:

Based on the content of this document, draft a 1-page brief for General Manager · Operations & Marketing at Contoso Pizza Co. Structure: - Market headline (2 sentences, 1 number) - 3 competitor moves we must track - 3 implications for Contoso Pizza Co in the next 90 days - 1 question to put to the Executive Committee Tone: direct, board-ready, no jargon. Max 350 words. Use [PLACEHOLDER] for anything not confirmed in the research. End with assumptions made and one open question.

Exercise 4 - Menu Rationalisation with Copilot in ExcelCopilot in Excel - Edit only

The operations question. Which products should we stop selling so we cut food waste and free up prep time? Each ingredient only lasts two days. We answer it end-to-end with Edit with Copilot in Excel - no Chat, no analyst agent, just formulas, tables and charts Copilot writes for you.

4.1 - Rank products by sales - one prompt

Open Data Contoh.xlsx in Excel for the web. Go to the sales sheet. Open the Copilot pane and switch to Edit.

Task 4.1Edit with Copilot in Excel - sales sheet

Add a ranking column and highlight the bottom 20 percent

On the sales sheet, do all of the following: 1. Add a column called "Total Units Q1" that sums Jan, Feb and March unit sales per product. 2. Add a column called "Rank" using RANK on Total Units Q1, largest first. 3. Add a column called "Bottom 20 percent?" that returns YES for products in the bottom quintile, NO otherwise. 4. Apply conditional formatting to the Rank column: red fill for the bottom 20 percent products. 5. Sort the table by Total Units Q1 ascending so the weakest sellers sit at the top.

4.2 - Split channel performance - a new sheet

Switch to the sales mix sheet (lines 10 to 116 are hot promos - Copilot handles them separately).

Task 4.2Edit with Copilot in Excel - sales mix sheet

Create a channel view on a new tab

Create a new sheet called "Channel View". On it, build a pivot table from the sales mix sheet grouped by product, with columns for Takeaway, Delivery and Aggregator units. Add a total column and a "% Delivery" column. Apply a data bar to the total column. Sort ascending so weakest products come first. Add a note on row 1: "Rows 10 to 116 are hot promos - treated separately in Sheet Hot Promos". Then create another sheet called "Hot Promos" with the pivot restricted to rows 10 to 116 only, same layout.

4.3 - Cost per product and waste risk - another new sheet

Use the SRM sheet (ingredients per product, cost per ingredient) and the sub recipe sheet (total ingredients used Q1). Two-day shelf life assumption per ingredient.

Task 4.3Edit with Copilot in Excel - new "Waste Risk" sheet

Build the recommendation view Copilot will fill for you

Create a new sheet called "Waste Risk". Build a table with one row per product and the following columns: - Product - Total Units Q1 (from the sales sheet) - Rank (from the sales sheet) - Cost per Unit (calculated from SRM: sum of ingredient qty times ingredient cost per unit of product) - Ingredient Count (count of distinct ingredients per product from SRM) - Shared Ingredients Score (how many of this product’s ingredients are also used by top-selling products in the sub recipe sheet - higher means less waste if we drop it) - Waste Risk (HIGH if Rank in bottom 20 percent AND Shared Ingredients Score is low, MEDIUM if only one of the two, LOW otherwise) - Recommendation (DISCONTINUE, REVIEW, KEEP based on Waste Risk plus Cost per Unit above the median) Apply conditional formatting: red fill for DISCONTINUE, amber for REVIEW, green for KEEP. Sort by Waste Risk descending then Rank ascending.

4.4 - The final chart the GM will show the ops team

Task 4.4Edit with Copilot in Excel - Waste Risk sheet

One chart, one takeaway line

On the Waste Risk sheet, insert a scatter chart with Rank on X-axis and Cost per Unit on Y-axis, coloured by Recommendation (red, amber, green). Label the top 10 discontinue candidates by product name. Above the chart, add a one-line takeaway starting with "So what:" that names how many products we recommend discontinuing and the estimated ingredient cost saving per week if we do.
